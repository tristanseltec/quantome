# Mixtures & Interest

## Key ideas

### Mixtures as weighted averages

When you combine two solutions, the concentration of the mix is a **weighted average**:

$$c = \frac{c_1 v_1 + c_2 v_2}{v_1 + v_2}$$

> [!TIP]
> **Seesaw shortcut:** the volumes are in inverse ratio to the distances from the final concentration. Mixing 10% and 40% to get 20%: the distances are 10 and 20, so the volumes are in the ratio $20 : 10 = 2 : 1$ (twice as much of the 10% solution).

### Interest

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Simple: $\;I = P r t$
> Compound ($n$ times per year): $\;A = P\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}$

> [!WARNING]
> "6% compounded semiannually" means $3\%$ every half year, not 6%.

## Worked example

**Problem.** How many litres of a 50% acid solution must be added to 30 L of a 20% solution to get a 30% solution?

**Solution.** Distances from 30% are 20% → 10 and 50% → 20, so the volumes are in the ratio 50% : 20% $= 10 : 20 = 1 : 2$. With 30 L of the 20% solution, you need **15 L** of the 50% solution.
Check: $\dfrac{0.5(15) + 0.2(30)}{45} = \dfrac{13.5}{45} = 0.3$ ✓

## Practice

1. \$2,000 is invested at 10% annual interest, compounded annually. What is it worth after 2 years?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

$2000 \times 1.1^2 = 2000 \times 1.21 =$ **\$2,420**.

</details>

2. How many litres of pure water must be added to 12 L of a 30% salt solution to dilute it to 20%?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The salt is unchanged: $0.30 \times 12 = 3.6$ L. A 20% solution holding 3.6 L of salt has volume $3.6 / 0.20 = 18$ L, so add $18 - 12 =$ **6 L**.

</details>

3. How many litres of a 20% solution must be added to 5 L of a 50% solution to obtain a 30% solution?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Distances from 30% are 10 for the 20% solution and 20 for the 50% solution, so the volumes go as $20 : 10 = 2 : 1$. You need $2 \times 5 =$ **10 L**. Check: $\dfrac{0.2(10) + 0.5(5)}{15} = 0.3$ ✓

</details>

4. \$1,500 is invested at 8% simple interest per year. How much interest is earned after 18 months?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

$I = Prt = 1500 \times 0.08 \times 1.5 =$ **\$180**.

</details>

5. \$5,000 is invested at 6% per year compounded semiannually. What is it worth after 1 year?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Each half-year rate is 3% over 2 periods: $5000 \times 1.03^2 = 5000 \times 1.0609 =$ **\$5,304.50**.

</details>
