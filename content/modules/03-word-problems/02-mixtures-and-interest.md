# Mixtures & Interest

## Key ideas

### Mixtures as weighted averages

When you combine two solutions, the concentration of the mix is a **weighted average**:

$$c = \frac{c_1 v_1 + c_2 v_2}{v_1 + v_2}$$

> [!TIP]
> **Seesaw shortcut:** the volumes are in inverse ratio to the distances from the final concentration. Mixing 10% and 40% to get 20%: the distances are 10 and 20, so the volumes are in the ratio $20 : 10 = 2 : 1$ (twice as much of the 10% solution).

### Interest

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Simple: $\;I = P r t$
> Compound ($n$ times per year): $\;A = P\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}$

> [!WARNING]
> "6% compounded semiannually" means $3\%$ every half year, not 6%.

## Worked example

**Problem.** How many litres of a 50% acid solution must be added to 30 L of a 20% solution to get a 30% solution?

**Solution.** Distances from 30% are 20% → 10 and 50% → 20, so the volumes are in the ratio 50% : 20% $= 10 : 20 = 1 : 2$. With 30 L of the 20% solution, you need **15 L** of the 50% solution.
Check: $\dfrac{0.5(15) + 0.2(30)}{45} = \dfrac{13.5}{45} = 0.3$ ✓
