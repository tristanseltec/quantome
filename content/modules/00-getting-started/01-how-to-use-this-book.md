# How to Use This Book

**Quantome** is a free, open-source mathematics textbook in six parts:

| Part | For whom |
|---|---|
| I · GMAT Quantitative Reasoning | GMAT Focus Edition candidates (Quant and Data Sufficiency) |
| II–V · Biomedical AI | Students and researchers who want to understand ML algorithms and neural architectures well enough to design new models, ending with a capstone that predicts heart-disease risk from DNA |
| VI · Applied Clinical Mathematics | Medical students, doctors, nurses and pharmacists: doses, infusions, fluids, nutrition and growth |

Each part can be read in order, but every module stands on its own, so you can jump straight to what you need. The [Biomedical AI Track](../the-biomedical-ai-track/) chapter maps out Parts II–V.

## How chapters are structured

Every chapter follows the same rhythm:

1. **Key ideas**: the concepts and formulas you must know cold.
2. **Strategy boxes**: faster ways to get to the answer under time pressure.
3. **Common traps**: the wrong answers the test-writers expect you to choose.
4. **Worked examples**: full solutions, step by step.
5. **Practice**: try each problem first, then open the answer.

> [!TIP]
> Cover the solution of every worked example and attempt it yourself first. Reading a solution feels like learning; producing one *is* learning.

## Tracking your progress

Click **Mark chapter as done** at the bottom of a chapter. Your progress is saved in your browser and shown in the sidebar and on the home page. Press <kbd>/</kbd> anywhere to search.

## Contributing

Quantome is written by humans and AI agents working together, and every contribution is checked before it is merged. Found a typo, a wrong answer, or have a better explanation? Every page has a **Suggest an edit** link. Chapters are plain Markdown files with math written as `$x^2$`, so contributing needs no programming. See the [contributing guide](https://github.com/tristanseltec/quantome/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md).
