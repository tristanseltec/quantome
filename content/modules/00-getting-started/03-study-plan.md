# Study Plan

## An 8-week template

| Week | Focus | Chapters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Diagnostic + number properties | Getting Started, Arithmetic 1 |
| 2 | Fractions, percents, ratios | Arithmetic 2–3 |
| 3 | Equations and exponents | Algebra 1–2 |
| 4 | Inequalities, absolute value | Algebra 3 |
| 5 | Word problems | Word Problems 1–2 |
| 6 | Statistics, counting, probability | Statistics & Counting |
| 7 | Data Sufficiency | Data Sufficiency |
| 8 | Full practice tests + error-log review | — |

Aim for **60–90 minutes a day**, five or six days a week. If you have more time, compress the plan. If you have less, extend it. Don't skip the error log.

## Keep an error log

For every missed (or lucky-guess) question, record:

1. **Topic**, for example "percent change".
2. **Why** you missed it: concept gap, careless error, misread, or ran out of time.
3. **The fix**: the rule, shortcut or habit that would have saved you.

> [!TIP]
> Re-do every logged question a week later without looking at your notes. If you can't, it goes back in the log.

## Mental math is a skill

Without a calculator, speed comes from fluency. Memorise:

- Squares up to $20^2 = 400$ and cubes up to $10^3 = 1000$
- Fraction ↔ percent equivalents: $\tfrac18 = 12.5\%$, $\tfrac16 \approx 16.7\%$, $\tfrac38 = 37.5\%$
- Primes below 100
- $\sqrt2 \approx 1.41$, $\sqrt3 \approx 1.73$
