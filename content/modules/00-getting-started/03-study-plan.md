# Study Plan

## An 8-week template

Each week has a content block and a review block. Read the chapters, work the examples with the solutions covered, then do the Practice section of each chapter.

| Week | Focus | Chapters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Orientation, strategies, diagnostic | [How to use this book](../../getting-started/how-to-use-this-book/), [The GMAT Quant section](../../getting-started/the-gmat-quant-section/), [Problem-solving strategies](../../getting-started/problem-solving-strategies/), then [Number properties](../../arithmetic/number-properties/) |
| 2 | Integers | [Divisibility, primes and factors](../../arithmetic/divisibility-primes-and-factors/), [Remainders and units digits](../../arithmetic/remainders-and-units-digits/), [Fractions, decimals and percents](../../arithmetic/fractions-decimals-percents/) |
| 3 | Proportional reasoning and speed | [Ratios](../../arithmetic/ratios/), [Estimation and mental math](../../arithmetic/estimation-and-mental-math/), [Linear equations](../../algebra/linear-equations/) |
| 4 | Algebra | [Exponents and roots](../../algebra/exponents-and-roots/), [Quadratics and polynomials](../../algebra/quadratics-and-polynomials/), [Inequalities and absolute value](../../algebra/inequalities-and-absolute-value/), [Functions and sequences](../../algebra/functions-and-sequences/) |
| 5 | Word problems | [Translating words into equations](../../word-problems/translating-words-into-equations/), [Rates and work](../../word-problems/rates-and-work/), [Mixtures and interest](../../word-problems/mixtures-and-interest/), [Profit, discount and percent problems](../../word-problems/profit-discount-and-percent-problems/) |
| 6 | Statistics, sets, counting | [Descriptive statistics](../../statistics-and-counting/descriptive-statistics/), [Overlapping sets](../../statistics-and-counting/overlapping-sets/), [Counting and probability](../../statistics-and-counting/counting-and-probability/), [Probability in depth](../../statistics-and-counting/probability-in-depth/) |
| 7 | Data Sufficiency | [The Data Sufficiency method](../../data-sufficiency/the-data-sufficiency-method/), [Data Sufficiency by topic](../../data-sufficiency/data-sufficiency-by-topic/), [Data Sufficiency practice set](../../data-sufficiency/data-sufficiency-practice-set/) |
| 8 | Full practice tests | Two or three official practice exams under timed conditions, with an error-log review after each |

Aim for **60–90 minutes a day**, five or six days a week. Take one official practice exam in week 1 as a diagnostic, so you know where your time is best spent.

- **More time?** Compress weeks 1–6 into four and add a timed set of mixed questions every other day.
- **Less time, or a strong maths background?** Stretch to 10–12 weeks, or skip the Easy problems in topics where your diagnostic was strong. Don't skip Data Sufficiency: it is a question type, not a topic, and it needs its own practice.
- **Every week**, spend the last session on review: redo the questions in your error log that are due (see below).

## Using the difficulty tags

Every practice problem in Part I is tagged *(Easy)*, *(Medium)* or *(Hard)*. Use the tags to decide how far to go:

| Tag | What it tests | Target time | What to do if you miss it |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Easy* | One idea, applied directly | under 1.5 min | Reread the Key ideas section: this is a concept gap |
| *Medium* | Two steps, or one idea plus a trap | about 2 min | Log it, and check the chapter's Common trap boxes |
| *Hard* | Several ideas, an unusual angle, or a well-hidden trap | 2–3 min | Log it, and compare your method with the fastest one in the solution |

A good rule: once you get the Easy and Medium problems of a chapter right without help, move on, even if some Hard ones still beat you. Come back to the Hard ones in week 8. On the real test, the adaptive algorithm decides which difficulties you see, so you need both speed on the routine questions and a plan for the hard ones.

> [!TIP]
> Time yourself on every practice problem from the first week. Getting a Hard question right in 6 minutes is useful while learning, but on test day it costs you two other questions. Note both the result and the time.

## Keep an error log

The error log is the single most effective study tool in this plan. Every question you miss, guess, or get right too slowly goes into it. A spreadsheet or a notebook both work. Use these columns:

| Date | Source and question | Topic | Tag | Time | Why I missed it | The fix | Redo on |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 Mar | Ratios chapter, a practice problem | Ratios | Medium | 3:40 | Concept: added the ratio parts wrongly | Total parts = sum of the terms of the ratio | 10 Mar |
| 4 Mar | Strategies, Practice 4 | Rates | Medium | 1:50 | Strategy: picked numbers but tested only one choice | After picking numbers, test **all five** choices | 11 Mar |

For **"Why I missed it"**, choose one of four causes, because each has a different cure:

1. **Concept gap.** You didn't know the rule. Cure: reread the chapter section and redo its worked examples.
2. **Careless error.** You knew it but slipped (sign, arithmetic, copying). Cure: a habit, such as "write the target first" or "check that the answer is reasonable".
3. **Misread.** You answered a different question (for example $a$ instead of $a + b$). Cure: reread the last sentence before choosing.
4. **Timing.** You got it right but too slowly, or ran out of time. Cure: learn the faster method from [Problem-solving strategies](../../getting-started/problem-solving-strategies/).

> [!TIP]
> Redo every logged question a week later without looking at your notes. If you get it right in time, mark it done. If you don't, give it a new redo date. Questions you miss twice deserve a flashcard.

Once a week, sort the log by topic and by cause. If one topic dominates, revisit its chapter. If "careless" dominates, the fix is a checking habit, not more content.

> [!WARNING]
> Lucky guesses belong in the log too. A correct answer you could not justify is a wrong answer that hasn't happened yet.

## Mental math is a skill

Without a calculator, speed comes from fluency. Memorise:

- Squares up to $20^2 = 400$ and cubes up to $10^3 = 1000$
- Fraction ↔ percent equivalents: $\tfrac18 = 12.5\%$, $\tfrac16 \approx 16.7\%$, $\tfrac38 = 37.5\%$
- Primes below 100
- $\sqrt2 \approx 1.41$, $\sqrt3 \approx 1.73$

See [Estimation and mental math](../../arithmetic/estimation-and-mental-math/) for drills.
