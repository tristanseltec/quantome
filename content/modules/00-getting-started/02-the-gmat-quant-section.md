# The GMAT Quant Section

## Format at a glance

| Section | Questions | Time | Question types |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quantitative Reasoning | 21 | 45 min | Problem Solving |
| Data Insights | 20 | 45 min | Data Sufficiency, Multi-Source Reasoning, Table Analysis, Graphics Interpretation, Two-Part Analysis |
| Verbal Reasoning | 23 | 45 min | Reading Comprehension, Critical Reasoning |

That is a little over **2 minutes per Quant question**. No calculator is allowed in the Quant section. An on-screen calculator *is* available in Data Insights.

> [!NOTE]
> Always confirm the current format on the official GMAC website before test day. Exam details change from time to time.

## What is tested

The Focus Edition Quant section covers **arithmetic and algebra** applied to word problems. Pure geometry is no longer tested directly. The core content areas are:

- Number properties: integers, divisibility, primes, odd/even, remainders
- Fractions, decimals, percents and ratios
- Algebra: linear and quadratic equations, exponents, inequalities, absolute value, functions
- Word problems: rates, work, mixtures, interest, profit
- Statistics, counting and probability

## The test is adaptive

Question difficulty adapts to your performance. Two practical consequences:

- **Accuracy early matters, but so does finishing.** Unanswered questions at the end carry a heavy penalty.
- **Expect to feel challenged.** If questions feel hard, that usually means you are doing well.

> [!TIP]
> Learn to make a fast, educated guess and move on. Spending 5 minutes on one question costs you two others.

## Review and edit

You may bookmark questions and change up to **three answers per section** at the end. Use this sparingly: the time is better spent answering every question once, carefully.
