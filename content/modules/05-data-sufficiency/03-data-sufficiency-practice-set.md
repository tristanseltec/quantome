# Data Sufficiency Practice Set

This chapter is a test, not a lesson. It contains 20 original Data Sufficiency questions that mix every topic in Part I, ordered roughly from easy to hard, as you might meet them in the Data Insights section. Take it in one sitting, timed, after you have worked through [The Data Sufficiency method](../../data-sufficiency/the-data-sufficiency-method/) and [Data Sufficiency by topic](../../data-sufficiency/data-sufficiency-by-topic/).

## How to take this set

1. Set a timer for **40 minutes**: 2 minutes per question. Work on paper; don't use a calculator.
2. Answer every question in order. If a question passes 3 minutes, guess using the AD/BCE grid and move on. Mark it so you can find it later.
3. Write your answers in a list. Don't open any explanation until you have finished all 20.
4. Score yourself with the answer key below, then read the explanation for **every** question you missed, guessed or took more than 3 minutes on.
5. Use the self-diagnosis table at the end to decide which chapters to revisit, and add each miss to your error log (see the [Study plan](../../getting-started/study-plan/)).

> [!TIP]
> Run the same process on every question: rephrase the question, judge statement (1) alone, judge statement (2) alone while forgetting (1), and only then combine. Most DS errors come from skipping a step, not from hard maths.

> [!WARNING]
> Several questions in this set contain the classic traps: a C that is really A or B, an E that is really C, two statements that carry the same information, and hidden negative or fractional cases. If an answer feels too easy, check it against the trap list before moving on.

All questions use the standard answer choices:

| Choice | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A** | Statement (1) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (2) alone is not sufficient |
| **B** | Statement (2) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (1) alone is not sufficient |
| **C** | BOTH statements TOGETHER are sufficient, but NEITHER statement ALONE is sufficient |
| **D** | EACH statement ALONE is sufficient |
| **E** | Statements (1) and (2) TOGETHER are NOT sufficient |

Numbers are real numbers unless a question says otherwise.

## The questions

**1.** *(Easy)* What is the value of $x$?

(1) $x^2 = 25$
(2) $3x + 7 = 22$

**2.** *(Easy)* If $n$ is an integer, is $n$ divisible by 10?

(1) $n$ is divisible by 20.
(2) $n$ is divisible by 5.

**3.** *(Easy)* A car-rental company charges a flat fee plus a fixed charge per mile driven. What is the charge per mile?

(1) The flat fee is \$30.
(2) Renting a car and driving it 150 miles costs \$90 in total.

**4.** *(Easy)* What is the average (arithmetic mean) of $a$ and $b$?

(1) $a + b = 18$
(2) $3a + 3b = 54$

**5.** *(Easy)* How old is Maria?

(1) Maria is 4 years older than Tom.
(2) In 6 years, Maria will be twice as old as she was 11 years ago.

**6.** *(Medium)* If $n$ is an integer, is $n$ odd?

(1) $n^2 + n + 1$ is odd.
(2) $(n + 1)(n + 3)$ is odd.

**7.** *(Medium)* Is $x > 0$?

(1) $x^2 > x$
(2) $x^3 > x$

**8.** *(Medium)* By what percent did the price of a share change from Monday's close to Wednesday's close?

(1) The share closed at \$50.00 on Monday and at \$49.50 on Wednesday.
(2) The price rose by 10% from Monday's close to Tuesday's close, then fell by 10% from Tuesday's close to Wednesday's close.

**9.** *(Medium)* What is the value of $x + y$?

(1) $x^2 + 2xy + y^2 = 36$
(2) $x - y = 4$

**10.** *(Medium)* In a town of 200 households, each household owns a dog, a cat, both or neither. How many households own both a dog and a cat?

(1) 120 households own a dog and 90 own a cat.
(2) At least 20 households own neither a dog nor a cat.

**11.** *(Medium)* If $k$ is a positive integer, what is the remainder when $k$ is divided by 5?

(1) $k + 3$ is divisible by 5.
(2) When $2k$ is divided by 5, the remainder is 4.

**12.** *(Medium)* A firm has seven employees. Is their average (arithmetic mean) annual salary greater than \$50,000?

(1) The median salary is \$52,000.
(2) The lowest salary is \$51,000.

**13.** *(Medium)* If $x$ and $y$ are integers, is $xy$ even?

(1) $x + y$ is odd.
(2) $x + 2y$ is odd.

**14.** *(Medium)* Working together at their constant rates, machines A and B complete an order in 6 hours. How many hours would machine A take to complete the order alone?

(1) Machine B alone would take 10 hours.
(2) Machine A works at $\tfrac23$ the rate of machine B.

**15.** *(Hard)* If $n$ is a positive integer, is $n$ a multiple of 6?

(1) $n^2$ is a multiple of 12.
(2) $n^3$ is a multiple of 8.

**16.** *(Hard)* Is $0 < x < 1$?

(1) $x^2 < |x|$
(2) $x^3 < x$

**17.** *(Hard)* A cinema sells student tickets at \$8 and adult tickets at \$13. How many adult tickets did it sell for one screening?

(1) Ticket revenue for the screening was \$170.
(2) Fewer adult tickets than student tickets were sold.

**18.** *(Hard)* List P is $1, 2, 3, 4, 5$. List Q also contains five numbers. Is the standard deviation of Q greater than the standard deviation of P?

(1) The range of Q is 4.
(2) The average (arithmetic mean) of Q is 3.

**19.** *(Hard)* What is the value of $x$?

(1) $|x + 2| = 2x + 1$
(2) $|x - 3| = 2$

**20.** *(Hard)* If $n$ is a positive integer, is $n^2 - 1$ divisible by 24?

(1) $n$ is odd.
(2) $n$ is not divisible by 3.

As a rough guide to your score on this set: 17 or more right within 40 minutes means your DS process is solid; focus on speed and the Hard items in [Data Sufficiency by topic](../../data-sufficiency/data-sufficiency-by-topic/). ## Explanations

**1.** What is $x$? (1) $x^2 = 25$. (2) $3x + 7 = 22$.

<details>
<summary>Answer: B</summary>

(1): $x = 5$ or $x = -5$. Two values: not sufficient. (2): $3x = 15$, so $x = 5$. Sufficient. A square gives two roots; a linear equation gives one.

</details>

**2.** Is the integer $n$ divisible by 10? (1) Divisible by 20. (2) Divisible by 5.

<details>
<summary>Answer: A</summary>

(1): $20 = 2 \times 10$, so every multiple of 20 is a multiple of 10. Yes. Sufficient. (2): $n = 10$ gives yes; $n = 5$ gives no. Not sufficient.

</details>

**3.** Rental: per-mile charge? (1) Fee \$30. (2) 150 miles cost \$90.

<details>
<summary>Answer: C</summary>

Let the fee be $f$ and the rate $r$ dollars per mile. (1) gives $f$ only. Not sufficient. (2): $f + 150r = 90$, one equation in two unknowns (\$0 fee and \$0.60 per mile, or \$30 and \$0.40). Not sufficient. Together: $30 + 150r = 90$, so $r = 0.40$. Sufficient.

</details>

**4.** Mean of $a$ and $b$? (1) $a + b = 18$. (2) $3a + 3b = 54$.

<details>
<summary>Answer: D</summary>

Rephrase: what is $a + b$? (1) gives it: the mean is 9. Sufficient. (2): divide by 3 to get $a + b = 18$. Sufficient. The two statements are the same information, so the answer had to be D or E.

</details>

**5.** Maria's age? (1) She is 4 years older than Tom. (2) $M + 6 = 2(M - 11)$.

<details>
<summary>Answer: B</summary>

(1): relates Maria to Tom, whose age is unknown. Not sufficient. (2): $M + 6 = 2M - 22$, so $M = 28$. Sufficient. The trap is to assume you need Tom's age, because statement (1) mentions him.

</details>

**6.** Is the integer $n$ odd? (1) $n^2 + n + 1$ is odd. (2) $(n + 1)(n + 3)$ is odd.

<details>
<summary>Answer: B</summary>

(1): $n^2 + n = n(n + 1)$ is always even, so $n^2 + n + 1$ is always odd. The statement is true for every integer and says nothing: $n = 1$ (yes), $n = 2$ (no). Not sufficient.

(2): a product is odd only if both factors are odd. So $n + 1$ is odd, which means $n$ is even. A definite **no**. Sufficient.

</details>

**7.** Is $x > 0$? (1) $x^2 > x$. (2) $x^3 > x$.

<details>
<summary>Answer: E</summary>

(1): $x(x - 1) > 0$ means $x < 0$ or $x > 1$. Not sufficient. (2): $x(x - 1)(x + 1) > 0$ means $-1 < x < 0$ or $x > 1$. Not sufficient.

Together: the intersection is $-1 < x < 0$ or $x > 1$. $x = 2$ gives yes. $x = -\tfrac12$: $\tfrac14 > -\tfrac12$ and $-\tfrac18 > -\tfrac12$, so both statements hold, and $x$ is negative: no. Not sufficient. Only a negative fraction exposes this, which is why testing fractions matters.

</details>

**8.** Percent change from Monday to Wednesday? (1) \$50.00 and \$49.50. (2) +10%, then −10%.

<details>
<summary>Answer: D</summary>

(1): the change is $-\$0.50$ on \$50, which is $-1\%$. Sufficient. (2): the overall factor is $1.1 \times 0.9 = 0.99$, a 1% decrease, whatever Monday's price was. Sufficient.

The trap in (2) is to think "+10% and −10% cancel" and treat it as a 0% change (still sufficient, but wrong), or to think you need an actual price. Percent questions need a base only when they ask for an amount.

</details>

**9.** What is $x + y$? (1) $(x + y)^2 = 36$. (2) $x - y = 4$.

<details>
<summary>Answer: E</summary>

(1): $x^2 + 2xy + y^2 = (x + y)^2 = 36$, so $x + y = 6$ or $x + y = -6$. Not sufficient. (2): $x = 5, y = 1$ gives 6; $x = 4, y = 0$ gives 4. Not sufficient.

Together: $x + y = 6$ with $x - y = 4$ gives $x = 5$, $y = 1$. But $x + y = -6$ with $x - y = 4$ gives $x = -1$, $y = -5$, which also satisfies both statements. The sum is still 6 or $-6$. Not sufficient. The trap is C: "two equations, two unknowns", forgetting that (1) has two branches.

</details>

**10.** 200 households; dog and cat? (1) 120 dogs, 90 cats. (2) At least 20 own neither.

<details>
<summary>Answer: E</summary>

$200 = 120 + 90 - \text{Both} + \text{Neither}$, so with (1), $\text{Both} = 10 + \text{Neither}$. (1) alone: Neither could be anything from 0 to 80, so Both could be 10, 11, …, 90. Not sufficient. (2) alone: nothing about dogs or cats. Not sufficient.

Together: Neither $\ge 20$, so Both $\ge 30$. But Both could be 30 (Neither = 20) or 40 (Neither = 30). Not sufficient.

This is the E version of the overlapping-sets pattern: (2) gives a bound, not a value. If it had said "exactly 20", the answer would be C.

</details>

**11.** $k$ divided by 5? (1) $5 \mid k + 3$. (2) $2k$ leaves remainder 4.

<details>
<summary>Answer: D</summary>

(1): $k + 3$ is a multiple of 5, so $k$ is 3 less than a multiple of 5: $k = 2, 7, 12, \dots$. Remainder 2. Sufficient.

(2): check each possible remainder $r$ of $k$: $2r$ gives remainders $0, 2, 4, 1, 3$ for $r = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4$. Only $r = 2$ gives 4. Sufficient. (Examples: $k = 2$, $2k = 4$; $k = 7$, $2k = 14$.)

The trap in (2) is to halve 4, get 2, and stop without checking the other remainders. Here no other remainder works, because doubling just shuffles the five remainders mod 5. With divisor 6 it would be different: "$2k$ leaves remainder 4 on division by 6" allows $k$ to leave remainder 2 **or** 5.

</details>

**12.** Seven salaries; mean above \$50,000? (1) Median \$52,000. (2) Lowest \$51,000.

<details>
<summary>Answer: B</summary>

(1): the middle salary is \$52,000, so three salaries are at least \$52,000. But the three lowest could be tiny. Salaries of 10, 10, 10, 52, 52, 52, 52 (thousand) have a mean of about 34 thousand: no. Seven salaries of \$52,000 give yes. Not sufficient.

(2): every salary is at least \$51,000, so the mean is at least \$51,000, which is more than \$50,000. Yes. Sufficient. The minimum bounds the mean from below; the median does not.

</details>

**13.** Integers $x$, $y$: is $xy$ even? (1) $x + y$ odd. (2) $x + 2y$ odd.

<details>
<summary>Answer: A</summary>

(1): a sum is odd only when one number is even and the other odd. Then the product contains an even factor, so it is even. Yes. Sufficient.

(2): $2y$ is even, so $x + 2y$ odd means $x$ is odd. Nothing is known about $y$: $x = 1, y = 2$ gives $xy = 2$ (yes), and $x = 1, y = 1$ gives 1 (no). Not sufficient.

</details>

**14.** A and B together take 6 hours. A alone? (1) B alone: 10 hours. (2) A's rate is $\tfrac23$ of B's.

<details>
<summary>Answer: D</summary>

Together the rate is $\tfrac16$ of the order per hour. (1): A's rate is $\tfrac16 - \tfrac1{10} = \tfrac{5}{30} - \tfrac{3}{30} = \tfrac1{15}$, so A takes 15 hours. Sufficient.

(2): let B's rate be $b$. Then $\tfrac23 b + b = \tfrac53 b = \tfrac16$, so $b = \tfrac1{10}$ and A's rate is $\tfrac1{15}$. Sufficient. A ratio plus a total fixes both parts.

</details>

**15.** Is $n$ a multiple of 6? (1) $12 \mid n^2$. (2) $8 \mid n^3$.

<details>
<summary>Answer: A</summary>

(1): $12 = 2^2 \times 3$. If $n^2$ is divisible by the prime 3, then $n$ is divisible by 3. If $n^2$ is divisible by 2, then $n$ is divisible by 2. So $n$ is divisible by 6. Yes. Sufficient. (Check: $n = 6$ gives $n^2 = 36 = 3 \times 12$.)

(2): $n^3$ divisible by 8 means $n$ is even ($n = 2$ gives $n^3 = 8$). But $n = 2$ is not a multiple of 6 (no), while $n = 6$ is (yes). Not sufficient.

</details>

**16.** Is $0 < x < 1$? (1) $x^2 < |x|$. (2) $x^3 < x$.

<details>
<summary>Answer: C</summary>

(1): $|x|^2 < |x|$ means $0 < |x| < 1$, so $-1 < x < 1$ and $x \ne 0$. $x = \tfrac12$ gives yes; $x = -\tfrac12$ gives no. Not sufficient.

(2): $x(x - 1)(x + 1) < 0$ means $x < -1$ or $0 < x < 1$. $x = \tfrac12$ gives yes; $x = -2$ gives no. Not sufficient.

Together: $-1 < x < 1$ (with $x \ne 0$) intersected with "$x < -1$ or $0 < x < 1$" leaves only $0 < x < 1$. Yes. Sufficient.

</details>

**17.** Tickets at \$8 and \$13. Adult tickets? (1) Revenue \$170. (2) Fewer adult than student tickets.

<details>
<summary>Answer: C</summary>

(1): $8s + 13a = 170$ with $s, a$ non-negative integers. $a$ can be at most 13, and $170 - 13a$ must be a multiple of 8. Checking $a = 0, 1, \dots, 13$, this happens only for $a = 2$ ($170 - 26 = 144 = 8 \times 18$) and $a = 10$ ($170 - 130 = 40 = 8 \times 5$). Two solutions: $(s, a) = (18, 2)$ or $(5, 10)$. Not sufficient.

(2): alone, clearly not sufficient.

Together: only $(18, 2)$ has fewer adult tickets than student tickets. So $a = 2$. Sufficient.

A quick way to do the check in (1): $13a$ must leave the same remainder as 170 on division by 8, which is 2. $13a$ leaves remainder $5a$, so look for $5a$ ending in remainder 2: $a = 2$ ($10 = 8 + 2$) and then every 8 more, $a = 10$.

</details>

**18.** P is $1, 2, 3, 4, 5$. Is SD(Q) > SD(P)? (1) Range of Q is 4. (2) Mean of Q is 3.

<details>
<summary>Answer: E</summary>

P has mean 3, range 4, and its squared distances from the mean are $4, 1, 0, 1, 4$.

Both statements describe P itself, so any list that matches P's range and mean satisfies both. Compare two such lists:

- $1, 3, 3, 3, 5$: mean 3, range 4. Squared distances $4, 0, 0, 0, 4$, total 8, less than P's 10. Smaller SD: **no**.
- $1, 1, 3, 5, 5$: mean 3, range 4. Squared distances $4, 4, 0, 4, 4$, total 16, more than 10. Larger SD: **yes**.

Both lists satisfy (1) and (2) together, so even together the statements are not sufficient. (Each statement alone is therefore not sufficient either.) The range fixes only the two extremes; the SD depends on where the middle values sit.

</details>

**19.** What is $x$? (1) $|x + 2| = 2x + 1$. (2) $|x - 3| = 2$.

<details>
<summary>Answer: A</summary>

(1): solve by cases, and check each solution.

- If $x \ge -2$: $x + 2 = 2x + 1$, so $x = 1$. This satisfies $x \ge -2$. ✓
- If $x < -2$: $-(x + 2) = 2x + 1$, so $-3x = 3$ and $x = -1$. But $-1$ is not less than $-2$, so reject it.

Also, $|x + 2| \ge 0$ forces $2x + 1 \ge 0$, so $x \ge -\tfrac12$, which already rules out the second case. Only $x = 1$. Sufficient.

(2): $x - 3 = 2$ or $x - 3 = -2$, so $x = 5$ or $x = 1$. Not sufficient.

The trap is to forget to check the case conditions in (1), keep $x = -1$, and conclude that (1) has two solutions (and then choose C, since 1 is common to both).

</details>

**20.** Is $n^2 - 1$ divisible by 24? (1) $n$ odd. (2) $3 \nmid n$.

<details>
<summary>Answer: C</summary>

Rephrase: $24 = 8 \times 3$, and $n^2 - 1 = (n - 1)(n + 1)$. Is this product divisible by both 8 and 3?

(1): if $n$ is odd, $n - 1$ and $n + 1$ are consecutive even numbers, so one of them is a multiple of 4 and the product is divisible by $2 \times 4 = 8$. But divisibility by 3 is not guaranteed: $n = 3$ gives $8$ (no), $n = 5$ gives $24$ (yes). Not sufficient.

(2): among $n - 1$, $n$, $n + 1$, one is a multiple of 3, and it isn't $n$, so 3 divides $(n - 1)(n + 1)$. But divisibility by 8 is not guaranteed: $n = 2$ gives $3$ (no), $n = 5$ gives yes. Not sufficient.

Together: divisible by 8 and by 3, so by 24. Yes. Sufficient.

</details>

## Takeaways

- Take practice sets timed and in one sitting; untimed DS practice hides your real weaknesses.
- A definite "no" is sufficient (question 6, statement 2).
- Two statements with the same information give D or E (question 4), and a statement that is true for every number gives no information (question 6).
- Squares, absolute values and quadratics hide a second branch (questions 1, 9 and 19). Find it, then check whether it survives.
- Negative fractions break many inequality "rules" (questions 7 and 16).
- Integer constraints can make one equation sufficient, or nearly so (question 17).
- Review every miss with the self-diagnosis table, log it, and redo it a week later.
