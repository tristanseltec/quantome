# Descriptive Statistics

## Key ideas

> [!IMPORTANT]
> $$\text{Mean} = \frac{\text{Sum}}{n} \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \text{Sum} = \text{Mean} \times n$$

- **Median:** the middle value of the sorted list, or the mean of the two middle values when $n$ is even.
- **Mode:** the most frequent value.
- **Range:** max − min.
- **Standard deviation (SD):** how spread out the values are around the mean.

### Standard deviation without formulas

You rarely compute SD on the GMAT. Know how it behaves:

| Change to every value | Mean | SD |
|---|---|---|
| add a constant $k$ | $+k$ | unchanged |
| multiply by $k$ | $\times k$ | $\times |k|$ |
| add values equal to the mean | unchanged | decreases |

> [!TIP]
> For evenly spaced sets (like consecutive integers), mean $=$ median $= \dfrac{\text{first} + \text{last}}{2}$.

## Worked example

**Problem.** The average of 5 numbers is 18. When one number is removed, the average becomes 16. What number was removed?

**Solution.** Sums: $5 \times 18 = 90$ and $4 \times 16 = 64$. The removed number is $90 - 64 =$ **26**.

## Practice

1. What is the median of $\{7, 2, 9, 4, 11, 4\}$?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Sorted: $2, 4, 4, 7, 9, 11$. The median is $\tfrac{4 + 7}{2} =$ **5.5**.

</details>

2. Which set has the larger SD: $\{1, 2, 3\}$ or $\{101, 102, 103\}$?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Equal.** Adding 100 to every value doesn't change the spread.

</details>
